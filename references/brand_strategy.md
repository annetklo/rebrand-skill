# Brand strategy: territories and the carrying idea (Fase 1-2)

Method file for the /rebrand skill. Read this before building visual territories (Fase 1, step 3) and before writing concept notes (Fase 2). Input for everything below is the approved creative brief from Fase 0, especially the personality sliders from ronde 2 and the scope decision (evolutie vs revolutie) from ronde 1. Never build a territory before the brief gate is passed.

---

## 1. The one-idea principle

A lasting identity is carried by exactly one nameable idea, statable in one sentence.
The idea is usually found by interrogating the name, the merger, or the category, never by styling: Paula Scher opened the Windows 8 project with "Your name is Windows, why is your logo a flag?" and that question WAS the concept.
Sagi Haviv (Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv) formalized the test: appropriate (right personality, and the less it says the better), distinctive (someone can doodle it from memory after one or two exposures), simple (works at 16px and on a building).
The one-sentence test is mechanical: write the idea as one Dutch sentence; if the sentence needs "en" twice, it is two ideas, reject it.
Expect volume before the idea appears: Lindon Leader ran hundreds of comps before the FedEx arrow landed; three sketches without a carrying idea is not exploration, it is decoration.

### Documented cases

| Merk (jaar, ontwerper) | Het idee in één zin | Why it worked |
|---|---|---|
| Citi (1998, Paula Scher, Pentagram) | De t in Citi draagt de rode Travelers-paraplu. | The merger's protection promise lives inside the name itself. Sketched on a napkin in the first meeting, fee $1.5M; her defense "seconds to draw, 34 years to learn to draw it in seconds" is the argument for concept over labor. |
| Mastercard (2016, Michael Bierut, Pentagram) | De twee overlappende cirkels ZIJN het merk, al het andere gaat eraf. | Identified the single equity element first, then subtracted toward it: bars, interlacing, and by 2019 even the word were removed. Reduction as concept, not restyle. |
| Chase (1961, Chermayeff & Geismar) | Een bewust abstracte achthoek rond een vast vierkant centrum, ontworpen om betekenis te verdienen in plaats van uit te leggen. | One of the first fully abstract US corporate marks. Proves an idea can be a stance rather than a picture: deliberate abstraction, chosen to unite two merged bank cultures under an unwieldy name, and left to acquire meaning through consistent use. |
| V&A (1989, Alan Fletcher, Pentagram) | Haal één poot van de A weg en laat de ampersand de dwarsbalk teruggeven. | Three characters fuse into one object. The idea lives in a typographic collision, not an added symbol. |
| NASA worm (1975, Danne & Blackburn) | Eén doorlopende lijn zonder dwarsbalken in de A's, zodat het woordmerk zelf ontworpen ruimtevaart is. | The reproduction constraint (the meatball was near-impossible to print cleanly) became the idea. Retired 1992 for sentiment, revived 2020: equity can outlive an official retirement. |
| Windows 8 (2012, Paula Scher, Pentagram) | Het logo is letterlijk een venster in perspectief, geen vlag. | Interrogate the name first: the strongest concept was already in it. One question reframed 25 years of flag logos. |
| I Love NY (1976, Milton Glaser) | Het hart vervangt het woord, zodat de kijker de zin zelf afmaakt. | A rebus sketched in a taxi during NYC's near-bankruptcy. Participation is why it spread: the viewer does the last step. |
| FedEx (1994, Lindon Leader, Landor) | In de witruimte tussen E en x zit een pijl vooruit. | Speed and precision smuggled into a custom Futura/Univers blend; invisible until seen, then unforgettable. Landed only after hundreds of comps, won 40+ design awards. |

### Idea classes (use as a generative checklist per territory)

1. **Naam-interrogatie**: the idea is already in the name (Windows, V&A, NASA). First question of any concept round: what does the name literally say, and does the current mark say the same thing? For Mission Relearn: interrogate "Mission", "Re-", and "learn" separately before touching form.
2. **Equity-reductie**: identify the one element people already recognize, subtract everything else (Mastercard). Only available in an evolutie scenario; requires naming the equity element explicitly in the brief.
3. **Synthese**: two things the brand unites, fused into one form (Citi, Chase). For MR this could be a fusion of education and technology, or of Mission Relearn and its method.
4. **Negatieve ruimte**: the idea hides in the counterform (FedEx). High risk of gimmick; passes only if the mark also works for someone who never sees the hidden element.
5. **Rebus / participatie**: the viewer completes the sentence (I Love NY). Works when the audience repeats or reproduces the mark themselves.
6. **Reproductie-constraint als idee**: a hard constraint (one continuous stroke, single color, 16px favicon) becomes the formal concept (NASA worm). Useful when the brief's top-5 toepassingen are unforgiving.

Every territory and every concept note must name its idea class. A direction that fits no class is styling, send it back.

## 2. Attribute-to-form translation

The personality sliders from the brief (discovery ronde 2) translate to form through this table. Grounding: angular marks measurably raise perceived competence, durability, and premiumness (Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services 2023); rounded marks raise perceived warmth and approachability (Jiang et al., JCR 2016); the Henderson/Giese/Cote line shows five measurable font characteristics (naturalness, elaborateness, harmony, flourish, weight) predict personality dimensions; warm hues pull toward energy and human proximity, cool hues toward institutional trust. Slider positions are tendencies, not binaries: a 70/30 position means the dominant cell wins but the minority cell may appear in one controlled place.

| Attribuut (NL) | Vorm en geometrie | Lijn en gewicht | Compositie en symmetrie | Kleurtemperatuur | Typografie |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| betrouwbaar | closed geometric forms on a visible construction grid | even, medium stroke, no modulation | symmetric, stable base wider than top | cool-leaning base, one warm accent max | geometric or grotesque sans, regular to medium |
| gezaghebbend | angular, corner radius 0 or minimal | heavier stroke, solid fills over outlines | strict vertical symmetry, centered lockups | dark cool base (navy, ink), palette of 2-3 colors | tight caps or small caps, high x-height sans |
| warm | rounded corners and terminals (radius at least 15-20% of stroke width), open apertures | medium, slightly soft joins | gentle asymmetry allowed | warm hues carry the majority of accent use (coral, orange) | humanist sans, open counters, no light weights below 400 |
| speels | mixed corner radii, one rotated or bouncing element | varied but never thin | deliberate asymmetry, diagonal energy | saturated secondaries next to the core color | rounded sans or one quirky glyph detail, never more than one |
| nuchter | flat single-color mark, no gradients, no shadows, no 3D | one uniform weight | quiet, grid-aligned, generous margins | near-monochrome plus one accent | workhorse grotesque, moderate spacing, zero decorative detail |
| pionier | forward diagonal or upward vector built into the mark | cut terminals, engineered joins | dynamic asymmetry pointing right or up | high-saturation accent on a neutral field | geometric sans with a single engineered oddity (cf. the worm's missing crossbars) |
| technisch | modular construction with repeating unit (dot, line, cell) | precise equal stroke, sharp joins | grid explicitly visible or implied | cool palette, blue-gray neutrals | geometric or monospaced candidate in the mix |
| toegankelijk | large counters, high aperture, exterior corners rounded | medium weight, never hairline | open spacing, nothing cramped | warm neutrals, high contrast for legibility | humanist sans, generous default letterspacing |
| premium / exclusief | angular, refined, small at scale | thin to light strokes, high stroke contrast | abundant whitespace, small mark relative to field | muted or dark palette, desaturated accents | high-contrast serif or light sans, wide tracking on caps |
| menselijk | organic irregularity, humanist axis, drawn rather than constructed | modulated stroke (calligraphic memory) | asymmetric, hand-placed feel | warm-dominant palette | humanist or semi-serif, true italics available |
| daadkrachtig | condensed, angular, forward-leaning | heavy weight, tight counters | compact lockup, high density | dark base plus one hot accent | bold condensed sans, tight tracking |
| zorgzaam | circles, ellipses, enclosing or embracing forms | soft even stroke | contained, centered, protective | soft warm tints plus one saturated warm | rounded humanist sans, ample line height |
| eigenzinnig | one deliberate rule-break (one altered letter, one broken alignment), rest strictly disciplined | consistent except at the break | the break is the focal point, everything else quiet | conventional palette so the formal break carries alone | standard typeface with exactly one custom glyph |
| kalm | horizontal emphasis, low contrast forms | light to regular, even | wide, symmetric, lots of air | low saturation, cool or neutral | light-to-regular sans, wide spacing, no bold in running text |

Usage rule: a territory is a coherent, internally consistent set of cells from this table. Pick the 3-4 attributes the brief scored highest, read their rows, and resolve conflicts explicitly (warm says rounded, gezaghebbend says angular: the slider position between them decides which one owns geometry and which one gets a single controlled appearance, for example rounded forms but a strict centered composition). Never average cells into mush; choose.

## 3. Visual territories (Fase 1)

A territory is a conceptual direction, one level above a moodboard: it has a name, a carrying idea, and implications for type, color, form, and photography. Keep the fidelity ladder straight (distinction via SOAK and Chris Do's stylescape method): a **moodboard** is pulled reference work by others and answers only "wijzen we dezelfde kant op?"; a **stylescape** is a curated composition with specific typefaces, color, and in-context mockups that functions as a visual contract; a **territory** is the conceptual layer both serve. In Fase 1 we present territories at moodboard fidelity; Fase 3 raises the chosen one to real design.

### How many, and how differentiated

Build 2-3, never more. Choose the differentiation scheme based on the brief:

- **Intensiteits-as** (The Futur's Hot / Medium / Mild): same strategy, three levels of daring. Use when the Fase 0 positioning is settled and the real question is how far to push. Maps naturally onto the evolutie-vs-revolutie answer: Mild = refine the current mark, Medium = same idea rebuilt, Hot = new carrying idea.
- **Concept-as** (Koto's parallel routes, e.g. their Glassdoor rebrand): each territory amplifies a DIFFERENT aspect of the strategy. Use when the brief itself has multiple credible readings (for MR plausibly: the educator reading, the technologist reading, the mission reading). Never mix the schemes in one round; it makes the choice incomparable.

### Naming pattern

Each territory gets a short evocative Dutch working title of two words that summarizes the carrying idea. Never "Optie A", never adjective soup like "modern/strak". Test: the name must work in the sentence "In het territory '...' zou het logo ...". Good: "Warme Wetenschap", "Helder Instituut", "De Vonk", "Stille Precisie". Bad: "Richting 2", "Clean & Bold".

### Required contents per territory (all at EQUAL fidelity)

1. **Naam**: two-word Dutch working title.
2. **Het verhaal in één zin**: one Dutch sentence stating the carrying idea, passing the "en"-test. Example format: "Het merkteken is de baan die je opnieuw instapt."
3. **Ideeklasse**: which of the six classes from section 1.
4. **Moodboard**: 6-10 pulled references (other identities, texture, photography, type samples). Sourced via researcher subagents from the landscape scan, not generated imagery.
5. **Typografierichting**: 1-2 named typeface candidates or a precise class ("geometric sans with humanist apertures, Cal Sans-adjacent"), taken from the matching cells in the translation table.
6. **Kleurstemming**: palette tendency and warm:cool ratio, plus explicitly what happens to coral (dominant, accent, or replaced; the brief's "is coral heilig" answer decides the allowed range).
7. **Vormtaal**: the specific cells from the translation table this territory commits to.
8. **Waarom dit past**: one line mapping to named brief answers ("scoort op de schuif warm 70/30 en op 'wat moet iemand in 3 seconden voelen': ...").
9. **Risico**: one honest line on what this direction sacrifices. Every territory has a cost; a territory without a named cost is a sales pitch.

The equal-fidelity rule is hard: an underbaked third option reads as a decoy and the client will notice. If you cannot bring a third territory to the same level, present two.

### Presenting for the choice (the Fase 1 gate)

1. Build one HTML gate page and open it as a local review surface (de HTML in de browser). Match the design language of the engagement's earlier exploration pages in <engagement-map>/ so the rounds read as one series: all territories side by side, identical layout per territory, the nine elements above visible for each.
2. After the client has seen the page, run one AskUserQuestion call: one option per territory, each option's description carrying the one-line story plus the risk line, phrased in Dutch. The client can always answer free-form (Other), including "geen van deze".
3. Record the choice and its motivation in the decision log. If the client rejects all territories, do NOT patch the losers; go back to the brief, ask which slider or answer the territories misread, and rebuild.

## 4. Concept note (Fase 2)

For the chosen territory (or the top two, if the gate ended in a tie) write one concept note each. The note is the contract for Fase 3: the form engine gets fed this note, not a loose description. Template, client-facing fields in Dutch:

```markdown
## Concept: <werktitel>

**Het idee in één zin:** <één Nederlandse zin, geen dubbele "en">

**Ideeklasse:** <naam-interrogatie | equity-reductie | synthese | negatieve ruimte | rebus | reproductie-constraint>

**Waarom dit bij de brief past:**
- <verwijs naar een concreet brief-antwoord, bijv. "Ronde 1: 'de raket mag evolueren, de beweging moet blijven'">
- <verwijs naar een schuifpositie, bijv. "warm↔technisch op 70/30 warm: vandaar ronde terminals">
- <verwijs naar de 3-seconden-vraag uit ronde 2>

**Doorwerking:**
- Beeldmerk: <hoe het idee de vorm bepaalt, incl. de gekozen cellen uit de vertaaltabel>
- Woordmerk: <hoe HET IDEE in de letters wordt uitgevoerd: case, spacing, en de chirurgische behandeling uit references/wordmark_typography.md. The note must state how the one idea executes at BOTH scales (full intensity in the mark, a precise echo or a deliberately argued alternative family in the wordmark). A concept note that bans the idea from the wordmark entirely fails review and returns to fase 2.>
- Kleur: <wat het idee van het palet vraagt, rol van coral>
- Taal: <hoe het idee doorklinkt in tone of voice en taglines, één voorbeeldzin>

**Haviv-toets:**
- Appropriate: <past de persoonlijkheid, en zegt het niet te veel? Verdict + één zin>
- Distinctive: <kan iemand het na één of twee keer zien naschetsen? Verdict + één zin>
- Simple: <werkt het op 16px, op een gebouw, in zwart-wit? Verdict + één zin>

**Kill-criteria (dit concept is dood als):**
1. <conditie 1>
2. <conditie 2>
3. <conditie 3>
```

### Rules for kill criteria

Each concept gets exactly three, and they must be observable conditions testable in Fase 3-4, not vibes. Draw from this bank and sharpen per concept:

- "Dood als het teken op een 16px-favicon niet meer als <het idee> leest." (the Haviv simple-test, made concrete)
- "Dood als een testpersoon het na één keer zien niet uit het hoofd kan naschetsen." (the doodle test)
- "Dood als het idee alleen werkt mét kleur: de zwart-wit-kopie verliest het." (color may support the idea, never carry it)
- "Dood als het custom letterdetail in het echte font niet te bouwen is zonder het woordbeeld te breken."
- "Dood als de één-zin bij het uitschrijven van de concept-notitie toch een tweede 'en' nodig blijkt te hebben."
- "Dood als het verborgen element (negatieve ruimte) uitgelegd moet worden om het merk te laten werken."
- "Dood als de fontlicentie voor de enige geschikte kandidaat boven <bedrag> uitkomt en er geen gelijkwaardig alternatief is."

A concept that trips a kill criterion in Fase 3 dies there, without renegotiating the criterion. That is what the criteria are for: they were agreed before anyone fell in love with pixels.

### Lineage kills

The client can also kill a concept at any gate with an answer that reaches further back than the artifact on the table: "begin opnieuw, terug voordat je X voorstelde" kills the entire concept LINEAGE, the concept plus every descendant variant built on it, not one variant. Record such a kill in the decision log as a lineage-kill, naming the root concept and the rounds it spans, but keep the artifacts: the client's favorites can resurrect "dead" work under a new reading, and a killed family may later turn out to be the source of the winning mark. After a lineage-kill, do not design again immediately; run the taste-recovery questions from SKILL.md first (what repelled, which earlier artifacts did land, must the mark carry the story), so the next round starts from a recovered direction instead of from more variants.

The Haviv-toets is the kill-gate before anything reaches the form engine or image generation: a concept that fails appropriate, distinctive, or simple does not get variants, no matter how good the sketch looks. Remember that paid image renders (for example the showcase renders of the logo-generator skill, github.com/op7418/logo-generator-skill, if installed) cost money and run only on explicit request; a failed Haviv-toets never earns a render.

## 5. Hybrid concepts: the warning

At the concept gate the tempting answer is often "kan het allebei? Concept 1, maar dan met de kleur van 2." Before agreeing to any merge, run these checks in order:

1. **Herformuleer de hybride in één zin.** Write the merged idea as one Dutch sentence. If it needs "en" twice, it is two ideas wearing one trenchcoat: refuse the merge as a concept merge and say so plainly.
2. **Onderscheid idee-fusie van ingrediënt-leen.** Merging two carrying ideas almost always kills both (a vault that is also an arrow is neither). Borrowing an ingredient is usually fine: the palette tendency of territory B applied inside territory A's idea, or B's typeface candidate, provided the borrowed cell does not contradict A's committed cells in the translation table. Name which of the two is being asked for before answering.
3. **Consistentie-check tegen de vertaaltabel.** If A committed to rounded/warm and the borrowed element from B is angular/cool, the hybrid contradicts itself and will read as indecision. Show the conflicting cells rather than arguing taste.
4. **Draai de Haviv-toets opnieuw op de hybride.** A merge that was not tested is a new, untested concept. It gets its own concept note, its own kill criteria, and it competes on equal footing; it does not inherit the pass of its parents.
5. **Lees het verlangen naar een hybride als signaal.** If neither concept convinced on its own, the fix is upstream, not in blending. Ask which specific quality is missing ("wat mist er in concept 1 dat je in 2 wél ziet?") and take that answer back to the brief or the territory round. Mastercard's lesson applies: the strong move is subtraction toward one idea, never addition of a second one.

If after the checks a legitimate ingredient-borrow remains, document it in the concept note under Doorwerking with one line naming the donor territory, so Fase 3 knows the borrow was deliberate.
